# Product Positioning

Positioning is the answer to "what is this and why should I care?" — framed specifically for your target user, against a specific alternative. Without clear positioning, marketing copy becomes generic, conversion rates suffer, and the product attracts users who don't stay.

## The Positioning Statement Template

```
For [target user]
who [specific need or problem],
[product name] is a [category]
that [key benefit or differentiator],
unlike [primary alternative].
```

**Example — invoice tool for freelancers:**
> For freelancers who lose time chasing late payments, Invoice Pilot is an invoicing tool that automatically sends payment reminders and late fees on your behalf, unlike generic accounting software that requires manual follow-up.

**Example — developer error tracking:**
> For engineering teams who spend too long debugging issues in production, Sentinel is an error monitoring platform that traces each error to the specific code change that introduced it, unlike Sentry, which requires manually searching through traces.

The template is for internal clarity — you don't necessarily publish the positioning statement verbatim. It becomes the source of truth that all marketing copy derives from.

## Finding Genuine Differentiation

Start with the competitive analysis from [core/competitive-analysis.md](../../product/competitive-analysis.md). Look for:

**Underserved segments:** The competitor serves enterprise; you serve small teams. The competitor serves technical users; you serve non-technical ones. A narrower audience served better is a genuine differentiator.

**UX/DX gaps:** The competitor is powerful but complex. You are simpler and faster to start with. Complexity vs. simplicity is a real and defensible differentiation when applied to the right segment.

**Workflow differences:** The competitor does X after you do Y; you reverse that order, which matches how your target user actually works.

**Absence of an integration:** The competitor does not integrate with a tool your target users depend on. You do.

**What genuine differentiation is not:**
- "Better quality" — too vague, anyone can claim it
- "We care more about users" — unverifiable
- "Simpler" without specifying simpler than what and for whom

## From Positioning to Copy

The positioning statement answers the "why you over the alternative" question for your ideal user. Use it to:
- Write the landing page hero headline (outcome or problem focus derived from the key benefit)
- Frame testimonials (select quotes that reflect the differentiated benefit)
- Guide ad copy (target the specific alternative's users with the specific differentiator)
