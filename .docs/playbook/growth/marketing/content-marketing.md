# Content Marketing

Content marketing builds an audience and drives organic discovery by publishing material that is genuinely useful to potential users — not promotional copy disguised as content. A solo builder with limited time needs a focused content strategy that produces compounding returns, not a high-volume publishing treadmill.

## Content Types and Funnel Stage

| Content type | Funnel stage | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Tutorial / how-to** | Awareness + consideration | Attracts users searching for how to solve the problem your product addresses. Demonstrates expertise. |
| **Educational blog post** | Awareness | Builds topical authority. Attracts people interested in the problem space. |
| **"X vs. Y" comparison page** | Consideration | Captures users actively evaluating solutions. Converts at a higher rate than pure educational content. |
| **Customer story / case study** | Consideration + decision | Shows the product working in a real scenario. Trusted more than generic claims. |
| **Definition / concept page** | Awareness | Ranks for "what is X" queries. Establishes authority. Good for AEO. |

## Funnel Sequencing

**Build awareness first.** Tutorial and educational content attracts users before they are in buying mode. This audience is harder to convert immediately but builds a relationship that results in conversion over time.

**Comparison pages convert.** A user reading "Invoice Pilot vs Bonsai" is already considering buying — they just haven't decided which product. These pages are worth more per visitor than awareness content.

**Case studies close.** A potential user who is 90% decided reads a case study to remove the final doubt. Prioritize case studies after you have a few happy customers willing to provide quotes.

## Sustainable Solo-Builder Publishing Cadence

More content published inconsistently is worse than less content published reliably. Search engines and audiences reward consistency. Recommended minimums:

| Resource level | Cadence | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Constrained (< 4 hours/week) | 2 posts/month | One tutorial + one comparison page |
| Moderate (4–8 hours/week) | 1 post/week | Mix of tutorials, comparisons, and posts |
| Available (8+ hours/week) | 2 posts/week | Add educational posts and case studies |

**Prioritize evergreen content.** A tutorial that solves a persistent problem will attract traffic for years. A "news" post about a product update will attract traffic for days. Allocate effort accordingly.

**Repurpose across channels.** A blog post becomes a LinkedIn post summary, a Twitter thread, a short video script. Writing the source content once and distributing in multiple formats multiplies reach without multiplying effort.

## Topic Selection

Start with the problems your first users mention. Their language describes what other potential users search for. A user who says "I kept forgetting to follow up on invoices" is naming a search query: "how to automate invoice follow-up emails."

**Keyword research tools for solo builders:** Google Search Console (free, shows what queries already lead to your site), Google "People also ask" boxes (shows related questions), and AnswerThePublic (maps question clusters around a keyword).
