# Launch Strategy

A product launch is not the day the product becomes available — it's a distribution event that results in a burst of awareness, trials, and feedback. The launch is most effective when it has an audience ready to receive it and a feedback mechanism ready to capture what happens.

## Pre-Launch: Building Readiness

**Waitlist landing page:** Before the product is ready, publish a minimal landing page that describes the product and captures email addresses. Even 100 emails is a meaningful launch audience compared to zero.

**Build-in-public audience growth:** Share the development journey on one social platform. Progress posts, decisions made, problems solved. An audience built before launch converts at higher rates than cold traffic — they've watched you build and have invested in the outcome.

**Community relationships:** Find 2–3 communities where your target users spend time (subreddits, Discord servers, Slack groups, forums). Participate genuinely for 4–8 weeks before launch. The community members who know your name will amplify the launch post; strangers will ignore it or report it as spam.

**Beta users:** Find 5–20 users to use the product before launch. Their feedback shapes the launch, their testimonials go on the landing page, and their voices in communities are more credible than the founder's.

## Launch Day: Distribution

On launch day, the goal is to reach as many potential users as possible through as many channels as simultaneously as possible:

**Product Hunt:** Submit at midnight Pacific time (posts go live at 12:01 AM PT). Announce to your waitlist and social following at the same time the post goes live. Ask for honest upvotes, not "please upvote" — Product Hunt penalizes coordinated voting patterns. Product Hunt is most valuable for SaaS products with developer or maker audiences; less valuable for niche B2B products.

**Relevant communities:** Post in the communities you've been a member of. Frame the post around the problem solved and ask for feedback, not upvotes. A "Show HN" post on Hacker News (for developer tools), a subreddit post, a Discord channel post.

**Personal network:** An email to friends, former colleagues, and contacts who are in the target audience or who know people in it. People help people they know; cold strangers don't.

**Waitlist email:** Send the launch announcement to every email collected pre-launch. Include a direct link to start a trial, a short product demo, and a request to share with someone who would benefit.

**Social broadcast:** Post the launch announcement across all active social accounts simultaneously with the other channels.

## Post-Launch: Feedback Collection

**Feedback must be active from minute one of launch.** Users who encounter a bug, a confusing flow, or an unmet expectation in the first 24 hours either email (if the email address is visible) or leave and don't return. A visible feedback mechanism captures that information:

- In-product feedback widget (Canny, Beamer, or a simple form)
- A public roadmap where users can vote on features
- A direct email address on the product (support@, hello@) with a short response SLA

The most valuable post-launch feedback comes from users who signed up and then stopped using the product — they tell you what the product didn't do that they expected. Reach out directly to churned trial users within the first week.
