# Social Media

Social media amplifies distribution and builds relationships with potential users — when done with focus. Posting mediocre content on every platform produces worse results than excellent content on one. The first decision is platform selection; everything else follows.

## Platform Selection

Choose based on where your target users actually spend time, not where you're most comfortable:

| Platform | Best for | Content fit |
|---|---|---|
| **X / Twitter** | Developer tools, indie SaaS, build-in-public | Short-form updates, threads, launch announcements, build-in-public |
| **LinkedIn** | B2B SaaS, professional tools, enterprise | Longer-form insights, product milestones, thought leadership |
| **Bluesky** | Developer / tech audience (growing) | Similar to X; growing dev community |
| **Reddit** | Products serving specific communities | Genuine community participation; not broadcast |
| **YouTube** | Products with demonstrable workflows | Tutorials, product walkthroughs, SEO via video search |
| **Instagram** | Consumer apps, design tools, creative products | Visual demos, behind-the-scenes, aesthetic-forward content |

**For B2B / developer tools:** X/Twitter or LinkedIn. Pick one for consistent posting; monitor the other.

**For consumer:** Instagram or TikTok if the product has a visual workflow; Reddit if there's a specific community.

## Content Types Per Platform

### X / Twitter — Build-in-Public Updates

- Progress milestones ("Hit 100 users today. Here's what I learned about onboarding:")
- Behind-the-scenes decisions ("Why we chose Prisma over Drizzle:")
- Short tutorials ("Three things I wish I knew about Stripe webhooks:")
- Genuine engagement: reply to conversations in your space

### LinkedIn — Insights and Milestones

- "Month 3 update: what's working, what isn't"
- Industry insights tied to your product category
- Customer stories (with permission)

### Reddit — Community Participation

Reddit requires being a genuine community member first. Post helpful answers for months before mentioning your product. When relevant, you can say "I built something that solves this" — but only when it's genuinely relevant to the specific question.

## Realistic Solo-Builder Cadence

| Platform | Minimum cadence | Maximum useful cadence |
|---|---|---|
| X / Twitter | 3× per week | Daily |
| LinkedIn | 2× per week | Daily |
| Reddit | 2–3× per week (replies) | As available |
| YouTube | 1× per month | 1× per week |

The minimum cadence is what's required for the algorithm to show your content to a consistent audience. Below that, reach effectively goes to zero. Above the maximum, quality typically drops.

**Batch content creation:** Write a week's posts in one session. Scheduling tools (Buffer, Hypefury, native scheduling) post them spread across the week.

## What Not to Do

- Post the same content identically across all platforms — each platform has different norms, formats, and audiences
- Post only promotional content — "Check out my product" with no value creates no audience
- Go quiet for two weeks then burst with ten posts — algorithms interpret inconsistency as low quality
- Argue in public — criticism of competitors or public disputes with users damages the brand
