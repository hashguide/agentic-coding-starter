# Competitive Analysis

Understanding what already exists is not optional research — it's how you find the gap your product can credibly own. Competitive analysis done well surfaces where incumbents are weak, confirms there's a market, and gives you the differentiation language that becomes the backbone of your positioning and landing page copy.

## Direct vs. Indirect Competitors

**Direct competitors** serve the same user, solving the same problem, in the same way. A user choosing between you and a direct competitor is making a direct substitution.

**Indirect competitors** solve the same underlying problem through a different mechanism or serve a different primary user. The user choosing between you and an indirect competitor is making a category decision.

Both matter, but they matter differently. Direct competitors tell you what the market expects as table stakes. Indirect competitors tell you what workarounds users are tolerating — those workarounds are your narrative hook.

**Example:**
- Building invoicing software for freelancers
- Direct: FreshBooks, Wave, HoneyBook (same user, same problem)
- Indirect: Excel/Google Sheets (same problem, different mechanism), PayPal invoices (same problem, different primary use case)

## Finding Genuine Differentiation

Differentiation is genuine if:
1. It matters to the target persona (from [user-personas.md](user-personas.md))
2. The competitor cannot easily replicate it without abandoning their current positioning
3. You can actually deliver it — it's not aspirational

Common false differentiators:
- "Better UX" (every product claims this; it's not differentiating unless you can be specific: "setup takes 3 minutes instead of 3 hours")
- "More affordable" (someone will always undercut you; price is a strategy, not a moat)
- "More features" (more features is not differentiation — specific, right-sized features for your persona is)

A strong differentiator: "The only invoicing tool that lets a client pay without creating an account." That is specific, verifiable, and appeals to the freelancer persona's pain point (clients who drop off at account creation).

## Feeding into Positioning

The differentiation you identify here becomes the raw material for [marketing/positioning.md](../growth/marketing/positioning.md). Specifically:
- The "unlike [alternative]" clause in the positioning statement comes from the direct competitor analysis
- The "key benefit" clause comes from the gap between what competitors fail at and what your persona needs
- The hero headline approach (problem-focused vs. outcome-focused vs. audience-focused) is often determined by what incumbents have claimed — position against the gap, not against the same space
