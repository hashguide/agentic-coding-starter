# Competitive Baseline

Use competitive research to understand current category expectations, terminology, workflow conventions, strengths, and recurring friction. The goal is an informed product baseline and differentiation, not reproduction of another product.

## Research set
Include direct competitors, adjacent substitutes, manual/current workflows, and strong products solving a similar interaction problem. Prefer current first-party product material, hands-on inspection, and credible user feedback when available.

## Capture
For each relevant comparison record:
- target user and positioning
- core workflow and time-to-value
- information architecture/navigation model
- important interaction patterns
- table-stakes capabilities
- pricing/packaging when it affects expectations
- onboarding and trust signals
- common friction, complaints, or gaps
- notable performance/mobile/accessibility expectations when observable

## Synthesize
Separate:
- category table stakes users will expect
- conventions that reduce learning cost
- weaknesses worth avoiding
- opportunities to simplify or differentiate
- features that are unnecessary for the intended segment

## Apply
Translate research into behavior-level requirements and product principles. Do not turn screenshots, copy, branding, or distinctive proprietary design into implementation instructions.

## Freshness
Record research dates and sources when the market, pricing, product capabilities, or technology can change. Revisit the baseline when positioning or a major workflow changes.
