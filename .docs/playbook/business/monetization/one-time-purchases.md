# One-Time Purchases

A one-time purchase exchanges a fixed payment for a defined deliverable: a download, template, course, game, license, physical item, or durable version of an application. It fits products whose value is delivered once and whose ongoing costs do not depend heavily on continued use.

## Offer Structures

| Structure | Best fit | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Single product | A focused asset or utility | Simple to explain, but limited expansion revenue |
| Editions or tiers | Different feature sets or usage rights | Captures willingness to pay, but adds comparison friction |
| Bundle | Related assets or products | Raises order value, but can obscure the core item |
| Perpetual version license | Desktop software or professional tools | Clear ownership; future development needs paid upgrades or support |
| Paid major upgrades | Products with meaningful version releases | Sustainable without subscriptions; fragments supported versions |
| Lifetime deal | Early financing or a deliberately bounded product | Creates indefinite obligations from finite revenue; use sparingly |

## Define What the Buyer Owns

State the deliverable, license scope, update policy, support period, device or seat limits, and refund terms before checkout. “Lifetime” must identify whose lifetime and which services remain included. If cloud features have ongoing cost, price them separately or cap them explicitly.

For digital goods, distinguish ownership of a copy from intellectual-property rights. Define whether commercial use, redistribution, modification, or client work is allowed. For physical goods, also define shipping regions, delivery estimates, returns, damaged goods, and inventory behavior.

## Pricing

Price against the value of the outcome and credible alternatives, not only production cost. Use tiers only when they map to meaningful buyer differences such as personal versus commercial use, feature depth, or included support. Avoid artificial feature removal that makes the base product incomplete.

Model sustainability before launch:

```text
contribution per sale = price - payment fees - tax - fulfillment - expected support - expected refunds
required monthly sales = monthly operating cost / contribution per sale
```

If every sale creates an indefinite hosting or support obligation, a one-time price is probably mismatched unless the margin funds that liability.

## Recommended Default

Start with one complete product and one clear price. Add a commercial tier only when license scope creates a real difference. Promise a defined update window rather than unlimited updates, and reserve paid major upgrades for substantial new value.
