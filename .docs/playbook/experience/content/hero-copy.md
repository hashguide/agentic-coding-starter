# Hero Copy

The hero is the first section of the landing page. Visitors decide within seconds whether to stay or leave — the hero headline and subheadline must answer "is this for me and does it solve my problem?" before the visitor scrolls.

## Headline Formulas

A strong hero headline is specific, benefit-focused, and requires no prior knowledge of the product to understand. Three formulas:

### Outcome-Focused

State the result the user gets. What does their life or work look like after using the product?

> "Ship your SaaS in days, not months."
> "Invoice clients. Get paid faster."
> "Stay on top of your customer relationships without the complexity."

**When:** Your product delivers a clear, desirable outcome that can be named in a sentence.

### Problem-Focused

Name the frustration the user is feeling right now. This creates immediate resonance with users who are in that pain.

> "Stop losing customers to slow support."
> "Tired of copy-pasting between spreadsheets and your CRM?"
> "Your analytics data shouldn't require a data team to understand."

**When:** The problem is well-known and painful enough that naming it is persuasive on its own.

### Audience-Focused

Name the specific user and what the product gives them. Narrows the audience but increases relevance for the intended user.

> "Built for solo founders who move fast."
> "The invoicing tool freelancers actually enjoy using."
> "CRM for sales teams that hate CRMs."

**When:** The product has a specific, well-defined audience that will immediately identify with the label.

## Subheadline Guidance

The subheadline expands the headline by:
1. Naming the specific capability or mechanism that produces the headline's outcome
2. Naming the audience if the headline doesn't
3. Removing ambiguity about what the product is

```
Headline:    "Ship your SaaS in days, not months."
Subheadline: "A complete library of playbooks for every stage of building and
              launching a SaaS — auth, payments, compliance, and beyond."
```

```
Headline:    "Stop losing customers to slow support."
Subheadline: "Help Scout gives your team a shared inbox, knowledge base,
              and customer data in one place — so you can respond faster
              and remember everything."
```

Length: 1–2 sentences. The subheadline is not a paragraph. The detail comes below the fold.

## CTA Button Copy Rules

The CTA label names the specific next action, not the abstract outcome:

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Submit" | "Create your free account" |
| "Get started" | "Start your 14-day trial" |
| "Sign up" | "Get started — it's free" |
| "Learn more" | "See how it works →" |
| "Try it" | "Try free for 14 days" |

**Rule:** State what happens when the button is clicked. "Get started" is ambiguous. "Start your free trial" is not.

**Free vs. paid:** If there is no cost to clicking the CTA, say so explicitly ("free trial", "no credit card required"). Visitors hesitate at calls to action that might lead to a credit card form.
